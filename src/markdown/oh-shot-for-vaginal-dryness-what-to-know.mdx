# OH-Shot for Vaginal Dryness: What to Know

Vaginal dryness is common and can affect comfort, sleep, exercise, and intimacy. It may happen during perimenopause, menopause, postpartum recovery, breastfeeding, after certain medications, or with underlying medical conditions.

For many patients, the first step is a focused evaluation of symptoms, hormones, skin and tissue health, and how much the dryness affects daily life, before any procedure.

## Common first-line options

Depending on your situation, care may include:

- vaginal moisturizers and lubricants,
- pelvic-floor support,
- prescription options when appropriate,
- behavioral and skin-care adjustments.

If symptoms remain disruptive despite these approaches, procedural options may be discussed.

## Where PRP-based treatment may fit

The OH-Shot uses platelet-rich plasma prepared from your own blood. It is considered for selected patients as one part of a larger plan, and it is not a first-line treatment for everyone.

Potential goals can include support for comfort, tissue quality, and sexual wellness symptoms. Outcomes vary, and no procedure guarantees a specific result.

## What consultation should include

A good consultation should cover:

- your symptom history and trigger pattern,
- prior treatments and response,
- medical history, medications, and contraindications,
- expected timeline and likely maintenance needs,
- realistic goals and documented risks.

If treatment makes sense for you, you should hear exactly what will be treated, what recovery usually looks like, and when follow-up is scheduled.

## What treatment day can look like

Most PRP-based visits are outpatient and involve:

1. blood draw,
2. PRP preparation,
3. targeted in-office application or injection,
4. post-care instructions.

Patients can usually resume normal routines quickly, but specific aftercare instructions should be followed closely.

## Safety and candidacy

PRP-based intimate procedures are not right for everyone. Whether you are a candidate depends on your history and goals. Before treatment, you should hear about both common and less common risks and get clear instructions on which symptoms need urgent follow-up.

## How this compares with other options

For some patients, non-procedural therapies are enough. For others, a combined plan offers better symptom control. The right plan is different for each person and often changes as symptoms and life stage change.

## Questions to ask before deciding

Use this checklist during consultation:

- What are the realistic goals in my case?
- What should I expect at 2 weeks, 6 weeks, and 3 months?
- How will we measure whether treatment helped?
- How often would I likely need maintenance?
- What are alternatives if response is limited?

## Deciding on the OH-Shot for vaginal dryness

The OH-Shot can be part of care planning for selected patients with vaginal dryness, but it should be evaluated alongside other evidence-based options. A thorough consultation and risk discussion are essential before deciding on any procedure.
